Salt & Powder

The West Indies and the coast of North America in the 1750s, from the Spanish Main to Louisbourg, as Britain and France slide into the Seven Years' War. You have a sloop, four guns a side, and no gold. Great Britain, Spain, France, the Netherlands and neutral Denmark hold more than a hundred ports, from cities to fishing landings. Sail as a trader, take a governor's letter of marque and hunt his enemies as a privateer, or take what you like as a pirate and answer to everyone.

Wind
You cannot sail into it. A broad reach, with the wind over your quarter, is fastest. Watch the compass.
Guns
They fire sideways. Turn your beam to the target. With aim assist on, the shaded lanes show where each broadside will fall and turn green when it will hit.
Shot
Round shot breaks hulls. Double shot hits far harder at half the range and is slow to load. Chain shot shreds sails so prey cannot run. Grape shot cuts down her crew before you board.
Raking
Cross her bow or stern and fire down the length of her deck: raking shot does nearly double damage and kills crew. Fire to leeward, holding the weather gauge, and your smoke blows clear of your gunners.
Boarding
Come within a ship's length and board. The fight is decided by numbers. Take the prize as your own ship, send her in to be sold, or plunder her and let her go.
Prizes
A ship strikes her colours when her hull is battered or her sails are shot away: white flag, sails furled, a white ring around her. Hold fire and sail into the ring to board for the full prize. Sink her and most of it goes down.
Fleet
Add captured ships to your fleet, up to four consorts, each under a captain you hire. Choose their formation and their orders: line of battle, engage at will, general chase, or hold fire. Lay ships up at your home port.
Career
Lawful prizes and commissions earn promotion in your patron's service, from lieutenant to commodore, with bounties, ships and dockyard rights. In wartime the trade sails in escorted convoys; neutrals may be stopped and searched for enemy cargo; captured officers can be ransomed or exchanged. Spanish guarda-costas search for smuggled cloth. Retire at your home port when you have had enough.
Privateering
In wartime a governor will sell you a letter of marque. His enemies' ships are then lawful prize: only they resent it, and hulls you send in are condemned by the prize court for more gold, paid at his ports or your home port. Take a ship without a letter and it is piracy, marked against you by every nation.
The war
The calendar follows history: Britain and France go to war in May 1756, Spain joins in January 1762, and peace comes in February 1763. The Spanish plate fleet, unarmed plate ships under a naval escort between Veracruz and Havana, is marked on the chart. From June to November, watch for hurricanes.
Ports
Cities and towns have governors, yards and prize courts. Small ports have a magistrate and a market. Landings have a few goods, no governor and no customs, and never turn anyone away. On the chart, small places are named as you zoom in. The Gulf Stream sets north-east along Florida and the Carolinas; expect fog on the northern banks and gales there in winter.
Course
Open the chart and click a port: the helmsman steers you there, round the land and against the wind. Touch the helm to take over.
Chart
Ports are the yellow marks on the small chart. Open the full chart, with every port named, from the panel at top left; the game pace is set there too.
